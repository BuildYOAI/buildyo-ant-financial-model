How this works

History. Six quarters (Q1'25–Q2'26) and three years of statements come from Anthropic's S-1, filed October 26, 2026. The balance sheet and cash flow are only disclosed at half-year points. Q3'26 revenue ($16.8B) was reported by Bloomberg, The Information and CNBC from an October investor presentation, consistent with the S-1's statement that Q3 revenue was 86× Q4'24. Compute is derived from the FT's reported $1.70 of revenue per compute dollar. The S-1 discloses a $2.0B donation-matching charge. The other Q3 lines are estimates.

Supply. Each compute contract the S-1 names (Google, Amazon, Microsoft, SpaceXAI, Lambda, Nscale, AMD, Broadcom leases, a planned Google TPU tranche) is a row with its disclosed size and dates. How spending ramps within those dates is an assumption, and one of the biggest swing factors.

Demand. Revenue grows quarter over quarter, with growth fading toward a long-run rate and slowing as it approaches a ceiling. Serving customers takes revenue ÷ serving efficiency (about 7×, matching the S-1's 85% first-half gross margin). Everything else is training and research. If demand needs more compute than is contracted, the model buys spot compute at a premium, up to a limit. Past it, training is squeezed first, then sales are lost, so new compute deals can add revenue. If contracts exceed need, Anthropic still pays, unless the resale response is on, in which case it resells the idle capacity at a discount. The price-war slider cuts prices while usage holds, so revenue falls but serving costs don't.

The IPO. Amazon's convertible notes ($6.2B face) convert into about 1.84B shares. The model marks them to the IPO price, a large non-cash loss, then moves them and the preferred stock into equity. Restricted stock units that vest on the IPO add about $30B of front-loaded stock comp. The IPO valuation and raise are press speculation, which is why they're sliders.

The analogs. Amazon overbuilt ahead of demand, nearly ran short of cash, and grew into its capacity. Cisco bought for growth that stalled, survived, and never regained its peak valuation. AOL's product kept working while the market moved on. The analogs describe the shape of each path, not its scale. None of these companies had Anthropic's revenue base.
